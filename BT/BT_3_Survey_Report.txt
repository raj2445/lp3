# SURVEY REPORT

## Types of Blockchains and Their Real-Time Use Cases

### 1. Abstract

Blockchain is an emerging technology that provides a decentralized, distributed, and secure method of storing and sharing digital information. It maintains records in the form of blocks that are connected using cryptographic techniques, making unauthorized modification of stored information extremely difficult.

Blockchain technology has applications beyond cryptocurrencies. It is increasingly being explored and implemented in industries such as banking, healthcare, supply chain management, education, agriculture, real estate, and government services.

This survey report studies the four major types of blockchain technology: Public Blockchain, Private Blockchain, Consortium Blockchain, and Hybrid Blockchain. It examines their working principles, characteristics, advantages, limitations, and real-world applications. The report also compares these blockchain types to understand their suitability for different industrial requirements.

### 2. Introduction

Blockchain is a type of Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) in which information is stored across multiple computers connected through a network. Instead of depending on a central authority, blockchain uses consensus mechanisms to validate transactions and maintain agreement among participating nodes.

Each block generally contains transaction information, a timestamp, and a cryptographic hash that connects it to the previous block. This structure provides transparency, data integrity, security, and traceability.

Bitcoin introduced blockchain technology in 2009 as a decentralized digital currency. Later, platforms such as Ethereum introduced smart contracts, allowing developers to create decentralized applications and automate business operations.

Today, blockchain is being used in several sectors to improve transparency, reduce intermediaries, prevent fraud, and increase operational efficiency.

### 3. Objectives of the Survey

The main objectives of this survey are:

1. To understand the fundamental concepts of blockchain technology.
2. To study the different types of blockchain networks.
3. To compare public, private, consortium, and hybrid blockchains.
4. To identify real-world applications of blockchain technology.
5. To understand the advantages and limitations of blockchain adoption.
6. To explore the future scope of blockchain in different industries.

### 4. Types of Blockchain Technology

Blockchain networks are generally classified into four major categories based on accessibility, participation, and network control.

#### 4.1 Public Blockchain

A Public Blockchain is an open and decentralized blockchain network that allows anyone to participate. Users can join the network, view transactions, and participate in transaction validation according to the network's rules.

No single organization controls the entire network.

**Characteristics:**

* Open access to all participants.
* High transparency.
* Decentralized network management.
* Uses consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS).
* Provides strong resistance to unauthorized modification.

**Examples:**

* Bitcoin
* Ethereum
* Litecoin

**Real-Time Use Cases:**

1. **Cryptocurrency Transactions:** Bitcoin allows users to transfer digital currency directly without depending on traditional banking intermediaries.

2. **Decentralized Finance (DeFi):** Ethereum supports decentralized financial applications that provide services such as lending, borrowing, and exchanging digital assets through smart contracts.

3. **NFT Marketplace:** Public blockchains allow users to create, purchase, sell, and verify ownership of Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs).

4. **Smart Contracts:** Ethereum enables automated execution of agreements when predefined conditions are satisfied.

**Advantages:**

* Greater transparency and decentralization.
* No requirement for a central authority.
* Open participation.
* High resistance to censorship.

**Limitations:**

* Transaction processing can be slower than some permissioned networks.
* Transaction fees may fluctuate.
* Public transaction data can create privacy concerns.
* Some consensus mechanisms require significant energy consumption.

#### 4.2 Private Blockchain

A Private Blockchain is a permissioned network controlled by a single organization. Only authorized participants can access the network, view records, and validate transactions according to assigned permissions.

It is commonly used by enterprises that require data privacy, controlled access, and efficient transaction processing.

**Characteristics:**

* Restricted access.
* Controlled by a single organization.
* Permission-based participation.
* Faster transaction processing in many implementations.
* Greater control over data visibility.

**Examples:**

* Hyperledger Fabric (permissioned blockchain framework).
* Enterprise blockchain networks.
* Private blockchain implementations using Quorum.

**Real-Time Use Cases:**

1. **Banking Operations:** Banks can use private blockchain networks to maintain internal transaction records and improve the reconciliation process.

2. **Healthcare Management:** Hospitals can use permissioned blockchain systems to maintain secure records of medical information and control access among authorized healthcare providers.

3. **Enterprise Data Management:** Organizations can maintain records of internal operations, employee information, and business transactions.

4. **Supply Chain Management:** Companies can track product movement, inventory records, and delivery information within their authorized business networks.

**Advantages:**

* High transaction efficiency.
* Better access control.
* Improved privacy.
* Suitable for enterprise applications.

**Limitations:**

* More centralized than public blockchains.
* Requires trust in the controlling organization.
* Limited public transparency.
* Network management depends on the organization.

#### 4.3 Consortium Blockchain

A Consortium Blockchain is a permissioned blockchain network managed by multiple organizations instead of a single organization.

Different organizations participate in maintaining the blockchain and validating transactions. It is particularly useful when multiple businesses need to share information securely.

**Characteristics:**

* Managed by multiple organizations.
* Restricted participation.
* Shared decision-making.
* Suitable for business-to-business transactions.
* Provides controlled transparency.

**Examples:**

* R3 Corda.
* Consortium implementations using Hyperledger Fabric.
* Multi-bank blockchain networks.

**Real-Time Use Cases:**

1. **Banking and Financial Services:** Multiple banks can use a shared blockchain network to exchange financial information, improve reconciliation, and coordinate transactions.

2. **Supply Chain Networks:** Manufacturers, suppliers, distributors, and retailers can share product information to improve traceability and transparency.

3. **Trade Finance:** Financial institutions, exporters, and importers can share verified trade documents and reduce manual verification processes.

4. **Healthcare Information Exchange:** Multiple hospitals and laboratories can securely exchange authorized patient information.

**Advantages:**

* Shared control among organizations.
* Better privacy than public blockchains.
* Efficient transaction validation.
* Improved collaboration between businesses.

**Limitations:**

* Requires agreement among participating organizations.
* Governance can become complicated.
* Participation is restricted.
* Establishing and maintaining the network can be expensive.

#### 4.4 Hybrid Blockchain

A Hybrid Blockchain combines the features of public and private blockchains. It allows organizations to maintain confidential information within a private network while making selected information publicly accessible.

This approach provides flexibility between transparency and privacy.

**Characteristics:**

* Combines public and private blockchain features.
* Supports controlled data accessibility.
* Sensitive information can remain private.
* Selected records can be made publicly verifiable.
* Flexible network management.

**Examples:**

* Hybrid blockchain implementations using Dragonchain.
* Enterprise systems integrating private blockchain records with public blockchain verification.

**Real-Time Use Cases:**

1. **Healthcare:** Hospitals can keep confidential patient information in a restricted network while publishing verification proofs that allow authorized parties to confirm record integrity.

2. **Real Estate:** Property ownership information can be maintained in a private system, while selected ownership verification records can be made publicly accessible.

3. **Government Services:** Government organizations can maintain confidential administrative information while allowing citizens to verify selected public records.

4. **Supply Chain Management:** Companies can keep sensitive business information private while sharing product origin and authenticity information with customers.

**Advantages:**

* Balances privacy and transparency.
* Flexible access management.
* Supports enterprise and public applications.
* Enables selective information sharing.

**Limitations:**

* Complex architecture.
* Requires careful access management.
* Integration between public and private networks can be challenging.
* Governance and maintenance may be complicated.

### 6. Real-World Applications of Blockchain Technology

Blockchain is being adopted across multiple industries because of its ability to provide secure, transparent, and tamper-resistant records.

#### 6.1 Banking and Finance

Blockchain enables secure digital transactions without depending entirely on traditional intermediaries.

Applications include:

* Cryptocurrency payments.
* Cross-border payments.
* Decentralized finance.
* Trade finance.
* Digital asset management.

Example: Bitcoin enables peer-to-peer digital currency transactions, while Ethereum supports decentralized financial applications.

#### 6.2 Healthcare

Blockchain can improve the security, integrity, and sharing of healthcare information.

Applications include:

* Secure medical record management.
* Medicine supply chain tracking.
* Patient identity verification.
* Medical data sharing.
* Clinical research data integrity.

Example: A permissioned blockchain can allow multiple hospitals to verify authorized medical records without giving every participant unrestricted access to patient information.

#### 6.3 Supply Chain Management

Blockchain helps organizations track products throughout the supply chain.

Applications include:

* Product origin verification.
* Inventory tracking.
* Shipment monitoring.
* Counterfeit product detection.
* Supplier and distributor coordination.

Example: Food supply chains can use blockchain-based traceability systems to record information about product origin, processing, and distribution.

#### 6.4 Education

Blockchain can help educational institutions securely issue and verify academic certificates.

Applications include:

* Digital certificates.
* Academic record verification.
* Student identity management.
* Prevention of fake educational documents.

Example: A university can issue digitally signed certificates whose authenticity can be independently verified by employers.

#### 6.5 Agriculture

Blockchain can improve transparency in agricultural supply chains.

Applications include:

* Farm-to-market tracking.
* Agricultural product verification.
* Smart contract-based payments.
* Crop insurance processes.
* Agricultural trade records.

Example: Blockchain can record information about crop production, storage, transportation, and delivery to help buyers verify product history.

#### 6.6 Real Estate

Blockchain can improve property transaction management and record verification.

Applications include:

* Digital property records.
* Ownership verification.
* Property tokenization.
* Smart contract-based transactions.
* Fraud reduction.

Example: Blockchain-based systems can maintain tamper-resistant property transaction histories and support digital verification of ownership records.

### 7. Advantages of Blockchain Technology

The major advantages of blockchain technology include:

1. **Decentralization:** Reduces dependence on a single central authority.
2. **Transparency:** Allows authorized participants to verify recorded transactions.
3. **Security:** Cryptographic techniques protect transaction integrity.
4. **Immutability:** Previously recorded transactions are difficult to modify without detection.
5. **Traceability:** Maintains a chronological history of transactions.
6. **Automation:** Smart contracts can automatically execute predefined conditions.
7. **Efficiency:** Can reduce intermediaries and manual reconciliation in suitable applications.

### 8. Challenges and Limitations

Despite its advantages, blockchain technology faces several challenges.

1. **Scalability:** Some blockchain networks face limitations in transaction throughput.
2. **Energy Consumption:** Certain consensus mechanisms, particularly Proof of Work, require substantial computational energy.
3. **Regulatory Issues:** Blockchain applications may face legal and regulatory restrictions.
4. **Privacy Concerns:** Public transaction records may expose sensitive metadata.
5. **Implementation Cost:** Developing and maintaining blockchain infrastructure can be expensive.
6. **Technical Complexity:** Blockchain development requires specialized technical knowledge.
7. **Data Management:** Incorrect information entered into a blockchain cannot automatically be corrected simply because the ledger is immutable.

### 9. Future Scope of Blockchain Technology

Blockchain technology has significant potential for future development.

Some important areas include:

* **Artificial Intelligence Integration:** Blockchain can help maintain verifiable records of AI model activities and data provenance.
* **Internet of Things (IoT):** Blockchain can support secure communication and transaction records between connected devices.
* **Digital Identity:** Blockchain-based identity systems can support secure identity verification.
* **Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs):** Distributed ledger technologies are being explored for digital currency infrastructure.
* **Smart Cities:** Blockchain can support transparent management of selected public services and infrastructure records.
* **Web3 Applications:** Decentralized applications can provide new models for digital ownership and online services.

### 10. Conclusion

Blockchain technology is transforming the way digital information and transactions are recorded, verified, and shared. The four major types of blockchain—Public, Private, Consortium, and Hybrid—offer different levels of decentralization, transparency, privacy, and control.

Public blockchains are suitable for open financial applications and decentralized services. Private blockchains are useful for organizations requiring controlled access and privacy. Consortium blockchains support collaboration between multiple organizations, while hybrid blockchains provide a balance between public transparency and private data management.

The real-world applications of blockchain extend beyond cryptocurrencies into banking, healthcare, supply chain management, education, agriculture, and government services.

Although challenges such as scalability, regulatory compliance, privacy, and implementation costs remain, blockchain has the potential to become an important technology for secure digital infrastructure in the future.
